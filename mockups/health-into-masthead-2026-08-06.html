A · Fold it into the LIVE pill

One pill answers one question — can I trust what I am looking at? Connection and instruments are the same question asked of two subsystems, so they share a control. Healthy is unchanged from today; degraded grows a qualifier and turns amber. Click → Notifications, where the sentence and the action live.

The risk, stated: it conflates two failures that need different actions. "The feed dropped" is reload; "a collector cannot read its store" is investigate. One amber pill for both means the operator cannot tell which without clicking. Mitigated by the qualifier naming the subsystem, not the severity.

B · A sibling pill that only exists when something is wrong

LIVE keeps meaning exactly one thing. A second pill appears only when instruments are degraded, carrying the left-edge signal rail this design language already uses for attention. On a healthy board it is not there — so its presence is the signal, and no space is spent being fine.

Connection and instruments stay distinguishable but share a footprint. Both states are always legible, so the operator learns where instrument health lives before it breaks — the discoverability problem the Clean up button already had.

My recommendation

B. It is the only option where a healthy board spends zero pixels on health, and where the control's mere presence is the signal — the same idiom as the Clean up button appearing only when there is something to clean, and the same reason the notification badge renders its zero rather than vanishing. It also keeps "the feed dropped" and "a collector is broken" as separate facts, which they are: one is reload, the other is investigate.

If you want health visible even when fine, take C — but that is a permanent claim of wellness, and this product's voice has been that silence means fine.
