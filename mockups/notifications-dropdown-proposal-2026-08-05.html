Notifications dropdown — proposal, revision 2

Two changes from the last pass. The dead-time bars are gone — an unlabeled rail cost a full line per row and made you decode a picture to learn something the 1h 04m beside the title already says in words. That line now carries what the agent actually asked, standing rather than hidden behind a hover. And the board-marks card is cut; marks on the workboard are a real piece of work, but they are not this panel, and pairing them here only raised the question of what they were doing in a dropdown spec.

So every row is three lines and each one is a sentence: what stopped, where it is, what it is asking. Focus and Reply ride on the middle line rather than claiming one of their own.

The closed button — the badge ink is the verdict

This is the control that has to work when the panel is shut, so it carries a kind, not just a count. The label stays "Notifications" in every state — it names what the control governs, and it is the same word in the dropdown, the footer switch, and Settings.

Three states

Ember, filled. Two agents stopped and are waiting on you. The only state that earns a solid fill.

Amber, outline. The watcher flagged something. Nobody is blocked; it is worth a look, not a trip.

Grey, outline. Nothing is waiting. The badge still renders, so a zero is a reading rather than an absence.

Ember never appears for anything except a person-blocker. That is the whole contract: if the button is red, someone stopped and is waiting for you.

Other states

A notification surface is judged on the day it has nothing to say. All clear does not go blank — it shows what the watcher is watching, because "watching, found nothing" and "not watching" are the two things an empty panel is otherwise ambiguous between. The eyebrow carries the verdict in the words the client already computes: Waiting on you → Watch → All clear.

What this commits you to

Two things the client does not compute yet. Dead time per stopped agent — how long it has been waiting on a person, which the 11m on each row and the standby total are both read from. A last-question excerpt for the peek line, which the drawer already reads but the strip never did; it is now standing content, so it has to be reliable rather than best-effort.
