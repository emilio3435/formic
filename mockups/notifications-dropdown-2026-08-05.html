Notifications dropdown — two directions

The inline findings ledger under the summary strip is deleted. Attention now has one home: the Notifications control in the masthead, backed by marks on the board itself. Both directions below start from the same diagnosis — the old ledger gave five rows identical weight when only two of them were actually blocking a human. Everything here is drawn with the console's own tokens; nothing new is invented for the palette.

The split both directions make: an agent that stopped and is waiting on a person is a queue with a running cost. Something the watcher noticed is a log entry. The old panel blended them, which is why it read as noise.

A. Standby queue — dead time is the metric

Leads with cost, not count: how much agent time is sitting idle on you right now. Rows are sorted by how long each has been stopped, and each carries a rail drawn to scale against the longest wait — the queue reads as a descending staircase, so the one bleeding the most time is unmistakable without reading a single number. Observations drop to a muted list with no rails and no buttons, because there is nothing to press.

B. Floorplan — the dropdown is a legend for the board

Groups by program, in the same containers the board uses, so the panel and the workboard beneath it are the same map at two zoom levels; hovering a group lights that lane on the board behind the panel. Its second move is the peek: the agent's own last sentence, inline, so a yes/no can be answered without ever opening the drawer. Observations still sink to the bottom, but they keep their program so you know where to look.

On the board — what carries the signal when the panel is closed

The dropdown can only be the whole answer if the board says where without being opened. Same vocabulary in both directions: an ember pip and a count on the program header, an ember signal rail on the specific stopped row. Nothing blinks, nothing fills.

The chip counts people-blockers only. A quiet lane gets an amber pip and no chip — it is worth a look, not a trip.

Closed button — the badge ink is the verdict

Ember = someone is stopped on you. Amber outline = the watcher noticed something, nobody is blocked. Grey = nothing is waiting; 41 agents are working.

Where they differ

A is faster when you are triaging by urgency across the whole fleet — it answers “what is costing me the most time right now” before you read a word. B is faster when you are working a specific program and want the panel to point at the board — and its peek is the only thing here that lets you answer an agent without leaving the panel. They are not exclusive: A's dead-time rail could ride inside B's grouped rows, at the cost of a busier panel.
